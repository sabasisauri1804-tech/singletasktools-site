Amount in Words: Spell Numbers in Contracts

This add-on writes the amount in words right after the figure in Google Docs™, the way contracts, promissory notes and invoices need it: “$1,500.50 (one thousand five hundred dollars and fifty cents)”, “1.500,50 € (mil quinientos euros con cincuenta céntimos)”, “$1,500.50 (Un mil quinientos pesos 50/100 M.N.)”. The figure stays. Six languages, ten currencies, one permission. Free.

How to use it

  1. Select the figures (a whole clause is fine), or just put the cursor on one.
  2. Open the Extensions menu, find Amount in Words and choose Amount in words, with currency and the currency, or Number to words (no currency). The words appear after the figure, in parentheses, after its currency symbol if it has one. Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on Mac) undoes it.
  3. No text yet? Insert example text, in the same menu, adds a paragraph with a few amounts, already selected.

What it leaves alone

  • Years (1900-2100), dates (20/10/2026, “20 de octubre”, “October 20”), percentages, times, ordinals (3ª) and numbers glued to letters or dashes (tax IDs, postcodes, CNPJ, CEP).
  • Numbers that follow words such as article, clause, section, law, decree, folio, page, No., C.P., RFC, CUIT, CPF, IBAN, account, phone.
  • IBAN, card and account numbers in groups of four digits, and phone numbers in groups of three (outside French, where the space is the thousands separator).
  • Figures already followed by a parenthesis, so running it twice adds nothing.
  • In a selection with several figures, the chosen currency applies to the ones that carry a currency sign or word; the others get plain words (“30 (thirty) days”). A single selected figure always gets the currency.

Options

Upper case, without parentheses, replace the figure instead of adding after it, «un mil» / «um mil» instead of «mil», cents as 50/100 (check style), and the language of the words. Ten currencies in the formats commonly used in each country: euro, US dollar, pound sterling, Swiss franc, Mexican peso (“50/100 M.N.”), Colombian peso (“M/Cte”), Peruvian sol (“y 50/100 soles”), Argentine peso (“Pesos … con 50/100”), Chilean peso and Brazilian real.

Languages

The menus and messages follow the language of your Google account: English, Spanish, Portuguese (Brazil, or Portugal if your account is set so), French (France, Belgium, Switzerland), German and Italian. The words can be written in any of the six.

Questions

Does anything leave my document?
No. The add-on is a script that runs inside your Google account and only asks for permission to edit the document it is open in. No servers of ours, no tracking, no account. See the privacy policy.
Can I trust the words in a contract?
The engine is tested against hundreds of cases in six languages, but always check the words: in a promissory note or contract the words prevail over the figure, and the final document is the responsibility of whoever signs it. The safest way is to select the clause with the amount, not the whole document.
Which decimal separator does it expect?
The one of the language of the words: comma for Spanish, Portuguese, French, German and Italian (1.500,50 or 1 500,50), point for English (1,500.50). When both separators appear, the last one is the decimal one.
Can I undo?
Yes. Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on Mac) removes what was inserted.
Does it work in the mobile app?
No. Google Docs™ add-ons run in the web version only.
