Equation Maker: LaTeX Math for Slides & Documents

An add-on for Google Slides™ and Google Docs™ by Single Task Tools. One task: turn LaTeX into a sharp equation image, right where you need it, and let you edit it later.

Got LaTeX from ChatGPT? Paste it in the side panel, check the preview, click Insert: the equation lands on your slide or in your document as a sharp image with a transparent background. Wrong sign? Click the equation, choose Edit, fix it and it is replaced in place. Google Slides™ has no built-in equation editor; Equation Maker gives you one. In Google Docs™ it adds what the built-in editor lacks: real LaTeX input, matrices and aligned steps, pasted straight from ChatGPT. Install it from the Google Workspace Marketplace™, or from Extensions › Add-ons › Get add-ons inside Google Slides™ or Google Docs™. Menus and messages in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German and Italian.

How to use it

  1. Open your presentation or document and choose Extensions › Equation Maker › Insert equation.
  2. Type or paste the LaTeX, for example \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}, and check the preview.
  3. Pick size and color, then click Insert.

What you can do

  • Side panel with a live preview: what you see is what gets inserted. Syntax errors are flagged before you insert, and ChatGPT’s $…$, \(…\) and \[…\] delimiters are removed for you.
  • Don’t know LaTeX? Ask ChatGPT or Gemini “write this in LaTeX: …” and paste the answer, or use the quick chips: powers and subscripts (x², v₀), fraction, root, Δ, ×10ⁿ, units, integral, sum, limit, matrix, system of equations, aligned steps, Greek letters, arrows and operators. Decimal commas stay tight (2,5).
  • Sharp on the projector and on paper, no blurry screenshots. Transparent background, the color you choose (fine on dark slides), size matched to your text (11, 14, 18, 24 pt or any size you type).
  • Google Slides™: the equation goes on the current slide, ready to move, resize or animate like any image.
  • Google Docs™: you choose. On its own centered line (the default) or inside the sentence, right where the cursor is. Short expressions (like x²) line up with the text; tall ones (deep fractions, integrals) rest on the text baseline as standard images, so use a block for those.
  • Google Docs™, the whole document at once: pasted text full of $…$ formulas? Click Convert all $…$ in this document: the panel counts them, you confirm, and every $…$ and $$…$$ is replaced by its equation image right where it was. Prices such as “$5 and $10” are left alone, and anything that is not valid LaTeX stays as text. Each converted equation counts as one toward the free monthly limit; up to 200 per pass.
  • Edit later: select an inserted equation, choose Edit selected equation, change the LaTeX and it is replaced in place. Correcting never counts against the free quota.
  • The panel stays open while you work: paste, insert, repeat. Your last 10 equations are one click away, and what you are typing is kept if the panel closes.
  • Your LaTeX is never lost: it is stored in the image’s description, so you can always recover and edit your source later. If you uninstall the add-on, or share the file with someone who does not have it, the equations stay in the file as normal images; only Edit needs the panel.
