Letter Case: Upper, Lower, Title & Sentence Case

A free add-on for Google Docs™ by Single Task Tools. One task: change the case of the selected text, letter by letter, so the formatting stays. Menus in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German and Italian.

Install it from the Google Workspace Marketplace™ (search for “Letter Case”), or from Extensions › Add-ons › Get add-ons inside Google Docs™.

Select the text, open Extensions › Letter Case and pick the case: UPPERCASE, lowercase, Capitalize Each Word, Sentence case, Title Case or tOGGLE cASE. The change is made letter by letter, so bold, italics, colors, sizes, links and highlights stay exactly where they were. With nothing selected, the word under the cursor changes. Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on Mac) undoes it.

The six cases

  • UPPERCASE and lowercase: every letter, accents included (á, ñ, ç, œ); the German ß is kept as it is.
  • Capitalize Each Word: the first letter of every word (“La Casa De Los Espíritus”).
  • Sentence case: a capital at the start of each sentence and the rest in lowercase. Words that already carry a capital in the middle of a sentence (Sevilla, García) and short acronyms (NASA) are kept as names, except in a sentence with almost every word capitalized (a title), which is lowercased entirely; in text typed all in capitals it cannot tell names apart, so check them afterwards. Common abbreviations (e.g., i.e., Dr., p. ej.), initials and “2.0” do not start a new sentence. If nothing changes because every capital looks like a name, choose lowercase first and then Sentence case.
  • Title Case: the English convention. Every word gets a capital except short words (of, the, and, de, los, y, du, der, della…), which stay lowercase unless they open or close the title or a sentence inside it: “The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King”, “La Casa de los Espíritus”. The language is guessed from the text itself (English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian). Note that in Spanish and Portuguese the academic rule for titles of works is a capital on the first word only; use Sentence case for that.
  • tOGGLE cASE: swaps the case of every letter, handy when a paragraph was typed with Caps Lock on.

Clean up colors

Text pasted from the web or from a PDF often arrives with a highlight (background color) or a text color. Clean up colors removes the highlighting, the text color, or both, for the selection only, and nothing else: bold, italics, sizes, fonts and links are untouched.

Limits

  • Up to 20,000 characters per run (about eight pages); for longer text, select it in parts.
  • Works on the text you select in paragraphs, lists and table cells.
  • No text yet? Insert example text, in the same menu, adds a paragraph ready to change.

Questions

Does anything leave my document?
No. The add-on is a script that runs inside your Google account and only asks for permission to edit the document it is open in. No servers of ours, no tracking, no account. See the privacy policy.
Why not the built-in Format › Text › Capitalization?
Google Docs™ offers UPPERCASE, lowercase and a Title Case that capitalizes every word. Letter Case adds Sentence case, a Title Case that keeps small words lowercase in six languages, tOGGLE cASE, and the color clean-up, and it never touches the formatting.
Can I undo?
Yes. Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on Mac) restores the text in one step.
Does it work in the mobile app?
No. Google Docs™ add-ons run in the web version only.
